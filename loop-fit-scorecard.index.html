The 10-minute disqualifier

  1. Is there an AI initiative — and did control/audit ever stall it? (Gate 1)
  2. Where does the portfolio & signal data live, and is it connected? (Gate 4)
  3. Who's accountable for letting an AI act — and are they in the room? (Gate 5)
01

Triggering AI pain Gate

The whole wedge. No agentic-AI ambition = nothing for the governance story to bite on.

  • Any AI/automation initiative touching planning, prioritization, or resource decisions? Where is it — idea, pilot, stalled, production?
  • If you piloted agentic tooling, what stopped it? Was control, auditability, or trust ever the blocker?
  • When AI suggests changing a priority, date, or allocation, who's allowed to say yes — and how do they defend it afterward?
  • Board/exec pressure to "use AI" with no governance answer?
Strong: a pilot that died at evaluation over control/audit, or an exec AI mandate with no governance answer.
Red flag: "we're not really doing AI" / "we just want a better roadmap tool" — differentiation gone, you're fighting Planview on price.
02

Portfolio scale & pain

Loop is high-ACV. The planning problem has to be big enough to justify the price.

  • How many concurrent initiatives are you keeping aligned to strategy at once? Across how many teams?
  • How do you plan and reprioritize today — and how often does it break or go stale?
  • How does new work enter the plan — real intake/triage, or does it just land?
  • Do you run on OKRs or a framework that prioritization ties back to?
Strong: large, messy, OKR-driven portfolio; manual reprioritization thrash; uncontrolled intake.
Red flag: small portfolio, a few teams, no strategy framework to anchor planning rules to.
03

Governance & approval structure

The audit log, rollback, and Dial only pay off if they have real approval and accountability needs.

  • Regulated industry, or subject to audit on operational decisions?
  • Defined approval thresholds today — who signs off on what, at what dollar/risk level?
  • Retention/audit requirement for decision records? (Loop = 7-yr immutable, exportable.)
  • If an automated action went wrong, what would the org need — gates, visibility, reversal?
Strong: real approval hierarchy, audit obligations, a named accountable exec. Regulated = governance becomes the reason to buy.
Red flag: no approval structure, no audit need — the governance machinery is overkill they won't pay for.
04

Data & integration surface Gate

No connected signals, no intelligence. The SE-critical fit check AEs routinely miss.

  • What systems hold portfolio, work, and delivery data — Jira/Atlassian, ADO, a PPM tool, spreadsheets?
  • Where would operational signals come from — execution tools, CRM, support, finance?
  • How clean and centralized is it, or fragmented across teams and tools?
  • Integration appetite and IT support to connect sources?
  • Identity/security: SSO provider (Okta/Ping/OneLogin?), SCIM, single-tenant, data residency?
Strong: centralized work data (esp. Atlassian), willing IT, SSO/SCIM needs Loop already meets.
Red flag: data scattered across 30 spreadsheets, no system of record — the signals layer starves and the pilot underwhelms.
05

Buying committee (two-sided) Gate

Bought by a pair: the operator who runs it and the exec accountable for letting it act. Missing either side stalls.

  • Who configures the planning rules and lives in the tool day to day? (the operator)
  • Who owns budget and the risk of giving an AI operational authority? (the accountable exec)
  • Who in security/compliance/IT holds veto power?
  • Has this group bought and deployed something similar together before?
Strong: engaged operator + accountable exec sponsor + a known security gatekeeper.
Red flag: a single mid-level champion with no exec air cover — the "advance the Dial to Delegate" decision never gets made.
06

Commercial fit (credit model)

Most likely place a good-fit deal dies at finance. Surface it early.

  • How do you prefer to buy — seat-based, platform, consumption? Any allergy to usage-based pricing?
  • Who approves a variable, consumption-based cost — and what would they need (caps, forecasts, predictability)?
  • Rough budget envelope for a platform like this? (Confirms the 6-figure motion is realistic.)
Strong: comfort with consumption pricing and budget at the right altitude.
Red flag: hard seat-based procurement mindset or no budget — fights Loop's "pay for intelligence, not seats" model.
07

Eval exit criteria

Define what the pilot must prove before you scope one.

  • What specific outcome would convince you — and your exec and security team — this is real?
  • Where would you start the Agentic Dial — Observe, Assist, or Delegate — and what advances it?
  • What does success look like 90 days in?
Strong: can articulate a concrete success criterion and a starting Dial position.
Red flag: can't define success — not ready for a pilot, ready for education.
